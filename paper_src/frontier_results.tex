Table~\ref{tab:frontier} and Figure~\ref{fig:frontier} give the sweep (75 generations per setting, seeds 1--5 of the 15 prompts; the reference loops in 29 of them). Both families reach zero observed loops, at very different distortion. A fixed price of $\lambda=3$ has 0/75 loops at 38.6 bits per generation, $\lambda=2$ has 2/75 at 18.5 bits (4/150 in the full run of Table~\ref{tab:primary}) and $\lambda=1$ has 4/75 at 5.0 bits. The loosest fixed budget tested, $\varepsilon=0.65$ (about 95\% of the reference's mean risk of 0.69), already has 0/75 loops at 1356 bits, 35 times the cost of $\lambda=3$ (prompt-bootstrap 95\% interval 33--37) and 73 times that of $\lambda=2$; the tightest, $\varepsilon=0.01$, costs 6364 bits, 23 times the cost of the strongest fixed price tested ($\lambda=12$, 277 bits). The budget rule does not become cheaper as the budget is loosened within the range tested, and no budget setting produced an intermediate loop rate, whereas the fixed-price rule's loop count and cost change smoothly with $\lambda$. Zero observed loops at both ends means a 95\% upper limit of 4.8\% on the true rate at 75 generations; the comparison is a comparison of cost at equal observed loop counts, not of loop probabilities.

The multipliers explain the cost, as in Section~\ref{sec:small}. The budget rule is inactive ($\lambda_t=0$) at 60--80\% of steps, and where it acts it uses large multipliers: a 90th percentile of 29--163 and a maximum of 115--437 (mean over generations, Table~\ref{tab:frontier}), against a single $\lambda$ of 2--12 for the fixed-price rules. The price of risk it pays varies by two orders of magnitude within a generation, which is the dispersion that Proposition~\ref{prop:waste} identifies with excess cost. Two limits apply. The recurrence risk is a proxy and not the hazard, so the quantitative law is not tested here. And the budget rule's loop count jumps from the reference level to zero between no control and $\varepsilon=0.65$; budgets between 0.65 and the reference risk of about 0.69 were not run, so the sweep compares the two families at the zero-loop end and not along a common frontier of loop rates.
